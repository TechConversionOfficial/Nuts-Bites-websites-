## The problem

A premium food brand has to communicate both **what the product is** and **why it makes a good gift or personal purchase**. A shopper might be looking for a specific nut assortment, a chocolate treat or a present for an occasion. Their next questions concern pack contents, size, cost, suitability and delivery.

If the website treats every product as an unstructured gallery, visitors struggle to distinguish everyday snacking from gifting. A useful storefront needs recognisable collections, convincing imagery, accurate product information and a direct path towards checkout or enquiry. It also needs to handle food information—especially ingredients, allergens and storage guidance—responsibly.

The available project records confirm the ecommerce brief, but do not include a dated audit of the previous website or evidence of a particular historical checkout problem. The challenge described here is therefore the business need behind the project, rather than a claim about an independently verified older implementation.

## How Tech Conversion approached the project

The portfolio identifies the project as a premium ecommerce presentation, and the saved client invoice documents a custom WordPress website engagement. The points below explain the appropriate design and content priorities for that brief. They are **not a claim that every feature was independently tested on the live site**.

1. **Put the products at the centre.** A premium food website should let product imagery, names and collections do the work, with supporting brand language kept concise.
2. **Separate snacking from gifting.** Product discovery should help visitors distinguish everyday nuts and chocolate treats from assortments intended for presents or celebrations.
3. **Explain each item clearly.** Product information should identify relevant pack sizes, contents, ingredients, allergen warnings and storage guidance where applicable.
4. **Keep the shopping journey direct.** Visitors should be able to move from a collection to product information and the next purchasing action without unnecessary detours.
5. **Make the brand recognisable.** The project's premium presentation should remain visually consistent and easy to distinguish.
6. **Consider mobile shoppers.** Category navigation, product images, prices and ordering actions need to remain usable on a phone. Independent responsive testing results were not available.
7. **Support trust before purchase.** Delivery, return, contact and food-related information should be easy to find and consistent with the seller's actual policies.
8. **Use the documented WordPress scope responsibly.** The original invoice identifies a custom WordPress website. It does not establish a specific ecommerce plugin, payment provider, inventory integration or fulfilment workflow.

## Standards the website should be reviewed against

The following are **appropriate evaluation references**, not claims of completed certification, testing or compliance.

- [Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals): evaluate mobile loading, responsiveness and layout stability on collection and product pages.
- [WCAG 2.2, Level AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/): assess keyboard navigation, accessible product controls, form labels, focus order, contrast and descriptive image alternatives.
- [Google Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials): review useful product content, crawlable categories, sensible URLs and appropriate metadata.
- [Schema.org](https://schema.org/): consider accurate product, offer, organisation and breadcrumb structured data where supported by the implementation.
- [India's Digital Personal Data Protection framework](https://www.meity.gov.in/data-protection-framework): review applicable handling of customer enquiries, account details and purchase information.
- **Food information:** check ingredient lists, allergen statements, labelling, applicable FSSAI requirements, product claims and storage guidance against the seller's verified records.
- **Ecommerce accuracy:** confirm that displayed prices, product availability, shipping information and return policies match the seller's actual terms.

No independent accessibility audit, performance benchmark, checkout test, food-labelling review or structured-data validation was supplied for this project record.
